How StockX and GOAT verify sneakers before they reach you

By the RestockRally desk

What StockX and GOAT say they check when a resale pair ships to them first, how long verification takes, why box condition matters, and what happens if a pair fails.

Last updated November 12, 2026 at 8:08 AM ET

When you buy a sold out pair on a resale marketplace, the main thing you are paying for besides the shoe is the platform's check that the pair is genuine and as described. StockX and GOAT both route many resale sneakers through their own facilities first. This guide sets out what each company says on its help pages. It is not a guide to spotting fakes yourself; for what a box label can and cannot tell you, see how to read a Nike box label.

StockX: inspected at a Verification Center

StockX says new, unworn items on its Verified Marketplace are either verified by StockX or ship directly from a StockX Verified Seller. When an item reaches a StockX Verification Center, the team inspects it against StockX condition guidelines before shipping it to the buyer. Buyers can also choose to send some Listings Marketplace purchases, such as used or unique items, for verification. Live Marketplace orders are not eligible.

On timing, StockX says items typically take one to two business days to inspect after arriving, and some need extra Quality Assurance review that can stretch to three to five business days. A seller's receipt does not speed this up: StockX says its check looks at the item, its container and its accessories, and proof of purchase does not factor in.

Why the box matters on StockX

StockX's guide to box condition requirements says every sale must include the correct brand issued box with its lid and a label showing the matching size and style code. A size 9 shoe in a size 13 box will not pass. A missing label means a fail. QR codes and RFID chips on the box must not be removed or obscured. Minor wear, such as a small corner dent, may still be accepted, but extensive crushing, a missing piece or a major rip likely will not. StockX notes the guide is not a final answer; only its inspection decides.

GOAT: ship to verify

GOAT's How does GOAT work? page says resale products, including sneakers, must ship to GOAT for verification first. Once verified, GOAT ships them to the buyer, and if a product is found to be inauthentic or not as described, GOAT says it will notify the buyer and offer a full refund. Retail products ship pre verified from partner stores, and Instant products ship from GOAT's own facilities.

GOAT's Assurance of Authenticity says its resale items and partners are verified or vetted by means such as digital authentication, in hand verification and machine learning. It says its authentication specialists go through mandatory training and inspect details from the label to the stitching, texture and color. For Ship to Verify orders that fail, GOAT says it may send the same item from another seller or give a full refund. For items shipped directly from a partner or seller, buyers report a problem through customer service or GOAT Assist, under its Buyer Protection Policy.

GOAT says authentication typically takes one to two business days after it receives a pair, with very unique or limited SKUs sometimes taking longer.

What this means when you buy

  • Check which route your order takes. On both platforms, not every listing goes through the same check. Read the shipping or verification option on the listing before you pay.
  • Confirm the style code. Match the code on the listing to the brand's own product page. Our guide to looking up a Nike style code shows how.
  • Compare with retail. Resale prices can sit above or even below retail. One recent example is the Air Max 90 Triple Black listed below retail before its release.

If you would rather pay retail, our restocks guide and raffles guide explain how to try for a pair at the store first. All our how-to pages are in sneaker guides.
