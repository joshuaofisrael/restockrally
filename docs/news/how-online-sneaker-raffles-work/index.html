How online sneaker raffles work at END., Foot Locker and adidas

By the RestockRally desk

What happens when you enter a sneaker raffle or draw: how END. Launches, Foot Locker's FLX entries and adidas CONFIRMED pick winners, charge cards and limit entries.

Last updated November 12, 2026 at 8:00 AM ET

A sneaker raffle, which most stores now call a draw or an entry, gives you a chance to buy a pair at retail rather than a promise of one. The rules differ by store, and the details matter: when your card is charged, how many entries you may make, and how a winner is picked. This guide sets out what three big release channels say on their own help pages. For Nike's version, see how SNKRS Draw and Line launches work.

END. Launches: one entry, charged only if you win

END.'s How to Enter for END. Launches page lists four steps. You pick the product and click Enter Draw, choose a size, a delivery address and a payment method, then tick a box confirming that if you win, the item will be dispatched and you will be charged automatically. The total cost is shown before you submit, and a confirmation screen shows your draw time.

END. says a confirmation email can take up to an hour, longer for busy launches. Winners are emailed within three hours of the draw ending, and app users may get a push notification sooner. Unsuccessful entrants are emailed too, though that can take longer.

The END. Launches Terms and Conditions allow only one entry per person per launch product. Registration details cannot be changed once you enter, and a purchase is valid only for the email, address, size and payment method you registered. People in the same household can enter, but each entry needs a separate END. account and a different payment method. Entries cannot be sold or transferred, and END. can disqualify anyone it finds tampering with the process.

Foot Locker: weighted entries in the app

Foot Locker runs release entries through its mobile app and FLX Rewards membership. Its help page on how to submit an entry says to sign in, open the Releases tab, choose the sneaker and size, then pick Ship to Me, Store Pickup, or both where offered. Ship to Me needs a valid card on file and a verified phone number. Store Pickup lets you choose up to three stores, but you can secure a pair at only one. After submitting, you can add Xtra Boosts.

Foot Locker's draw is not a pure random pick. Its page on selection criteria describes a weighted algorithm that takes into account the total number of entries, loyalty and FLX membership tenure, your FLX Rewards tier, and the Xtra Boosts applied by you and by other entrants. It also says an entry does not guarantee a reservation, even with Boosts, and that resellers, employees and people using bots are not eligible.

On limits, Foot Locker says you may reserve one pair per release per account, with one FLX account per person and two per household. You can enter on the Foot Locker, Kids Foot Locker and Champs Sports apps where available, but you can secure only one pair in total.

adidas CONFIRMED: raffle, queue or invite

adidas uses its CONFIRMED app for limited releases. Its CONFIRMED page says each raffle is open for a limited time, winners are selected at random, and if you do not win you are not charged. The app also uses first come, first served queues and invitation only releases. We explain those three formats in how the adidas CONFIRMED app works.

What the three have in common

  • No pair is promised. Each store says an entry is a chance, not a reservation.
  • One account per person. END. and Foot Locker both cap entries per person, and both say they can remove entries that break the rules.
  • Your details are locked in. Set the right size, address and card before you enter. END. says registration details cannot be amended after you register.
  • Enter through the store itself. Genuine draws run on the retailer's own site or app. Our guide to spotting a fake release ad or raffle covers the warning signs, and our raffles page lists draws only after we confirm them with the retailer.
