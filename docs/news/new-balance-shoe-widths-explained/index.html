New Balance shoe widths explained: what B, D, 2E and 4E mean

By the RestockRally desk

New Balance width letters mean different things in men's and women's shoes. Here is New Balance's own width list, from 2A to 6E, and how to tell if you need a different width.

Last updated November 12, 2026 at 8:06 AM ET

New Balance sells many of its shoes in more than one width, and the letter after the size tells you which. The catch is that the same letter means a different width in men's and women's shoes. This guide uses the width list New Balance publishes on its customer help pages in Australia and New Zealand.

New Balance width letters

WidthMen'sWomen's
4ANot listedExtra narrow
2AExtra narrowNarrow
BNarrowStandard
DStandardWide
2EWideExtra wide
4EExtra wideXX wide
6EXX wideNot listed

Kids' shoes use a separate scale: M for standard, W for wide and XW for extra wide.

Why the same letter differs

In New Balance's list, standard width is D for men and B for women. So a men's B is narrow, while a women's B is standard. A women's D is wide, while a men's D is standard. When you shop a unisex or men's sized release in a women's size, check the width letter as well as the length.

Signs you need a different width

New Balance gives three checks:

  • If the shoe feels too tight across the sides, you may need a wider width.
  • If your foot slips or feels loose, a narrower width may fit better.
  • A good fit should feel snug but not tight, with no pinching or pressure points.

It also suggests trying shoes on in the late afternoon, when feet are at their largest, making sure the heel is snug with no slipping, and leaving enough room in the toe box to wiggle your toes.

Widths on limited releases

Many limited and collaboration pairs come in a single standard width. Check the size run on the product page before you enter a draw or join a queue. Recent New Balance releases we have covered include the 990v7 Kool Grey, the Kith x New Balance 2011 Monuments collection and the Abzorb 1890 Altitude Blue Black.
